## ODYSSEUS (King of Ithaca)

**Age appearance**: Late 30s to mid-40s by the time of return (he left as a young father; twenty years have passed).

**Build**: Shorter than the tallest heroes (Agamemnon, Menelaus, Ajax) but broader in the chest and shoulders. Dense, powerful, compact strength rather than long-limbed elegance. Barrel-chested, thick arms and thighs from years of rowing, fighting, and surviving.

**Face**: Weathered by sun, wind, and salt. Strong brow, deep-set intelligent eyes that miss nothing. Full dark beard, often salt-stiffened or unkempt during the wanderings. When Athena restores him, the beard is thick and the hair grows dense and curling “like hyacinth petals”.

**Hair**: Dark brown to near-black, thick, curling when healthy. In the beggar disguise: sparse, grey-streaked, matted.

**Skin**: Olive-Mediterranean, deeply tanned and lined from twenty years of sea and sun. Scars visible — especially the boar-hunt scar on the thigh that Eurycleia recognizes.

**Voice / Presence (for sensory prompts)**: Deep, controlled, persuasive. When he speaks in the hall after the bow, the air changes.

**Key Visual States**:
1. Beggar disguise — old, bent, filthy rags, staff, hollow cheeks
2. Restored king — upright, powerful, hyacinth-curling hair, clean but still sea-hardened
3. Warrior at Troy / early voyage — younger, less lined, full battle readiness

## PENELOPE (Queen of Ithaca)

**Age appearance**: Mature woman in her late 30s to early 40s. Not a young girl. Beauty that has been tested by twenty years of waiting.

**Build**: Graceful, upright, queenly posture. Not fragile.

**Face**: Fine features, dark eyes that hold both sorrow and steel. When Athena enhances her before the suitors, a sudden immortal radiance of *kallos* makes the suitors’ knees weak, yet the underlying maturity remains.

**Hair**: Dark, thick, usually bound or simply arranged. Never the elaborate classical styles of later centuries.

**Skin**: Fairer than Odysseus from indoor life, but still Mediterranean. Slight signs of strain around the eyes and mouth from years of vigilance.

**Presence**: Quiet authority. Cunning intelligence visible in the set of her mouth and the way she watches.

## TELEMACHUS

**Age**: Approximately 20–21.

**Build**: Still filling out into his father’s broader frame. Taller than the average youth, but not yet the dense power of Odysseus.

**Face**: Clear resemblance to Odysseus in the eyes and brow. Youthful, still capable of uncertainty, but gaining steel.

**Hair**: Dark, thick, less weathered than his father’s.

**Presence**: Begins the poem as a young man who has never known his father; ends it standing beside him as a warrior.

---

## ATHENA

**When revealed**: Tall, grey-eyed (*glaukopis*), commanding. The aegis when worn is terrible and beautiful. Presence that makes mortals fall silent.

**In mortal guise**: Changes fully — Mentes (Taphian lord), Mentor (old Ithacan), a young shepherd, etc. The eyes remain the only consistent clue for those who know how to look.

## Supporting Characters — Quick Physical Notes

- **Eumaeus**: Older man, weather-beaten, loyal eyes, practical working clothes, the dignity of true xenia.
- **Eurycleia**: Elderly nurse, thin strong hands, sharp eyes that miss nothing, grey hair bound.
- **Antinous**: Handsome in a hard, arrogant way; the most dangerous of the suitors; dark hair, well-kept appearance that contrasts with his moral ugliness.
- **Nausicaa**: Young woman at the peak of unmarried beauty — fresh, dignified, not sexualized beyond Homeric propriety.
- **Calypso**: Immortal nymph beauty — ageless, radiant, but her island has a quality of suspended, slightly melancholy perfection.
- **Circe**: Powerful, beautiful, dangerous intelligence in the eyes; the mistress of her own domain.
- **Polyphemus**: Enormous, one-eyed, savage, pastoral giant; not cartoonish, but terrifying in scale and appetite.

## Sensory Realism Mandate for All Prompts

Every Grok Imagine prompt must now prioritize **human sensory realism**:

- Skin texture, pores, salt residue, sweat, dirt, scars
- Fabric weight and weave (linen, wool)
- Metal that looks heavy and used (bronze dark, not shiny fantasy gold)
- Firelight that flickers on faces and bronze
- Sea air, spray, wet hair, sand on skin
- Eyes that hold real emotion (grief, cunning, rage, longing)
- Atmospheric depth (dust in shafts of light, smoke, sea haze)

Avoid plastic, over-smoothed, or overly “cinematic” modern looks. The goal is the feeling that a human eye and ear could have stood in that moment.
