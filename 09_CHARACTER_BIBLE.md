## Odysseus
- Mature man (appears late 30s to mid-40s by return)
- Bearded, dark hair streaked by sea and sun
- Weathered, intelligent face, sharp eyes
- Strong, capable build — not exaggerated musculature
- When disguised: old, bent, tattered clothing, staff
- When revealed: king of Ithaca, bearing of authority and cunning

## Penelope
- Mature woman of dignity and quiet strength
- Beautiful but marked by years of waiting and vigilance
- Never a young girl; queenly presence
- Dark hair, composed expression that can shift to cunning or deep emotion

## Telemachus
- Young man in early twenties
- Growing into his father’s likeness and authority
- Initially uncertain, later resolute

## Athena
- When revealed: grey-eyed, commanding, often with aegis
- Frequently in mortal guise (Mentes, Mentor, shepherd, etc.)
- Protective, strategic, never sentimental

## Poseidon
- Dark-bearded, powerful, associated with the sea
- Wrathful toward Odysseus after the blinding of Polyphemus

## Key Supporting Figures
- **Eumaeus**: Loyal, older swineherd, embodiment of true xenia
- **Eurycleia**: Aged nurse, devoted, recognizes the scar
- **Antinous**: Arrogant leader of the suitors, first to die
- **Eurymachus**: Smooth-talking, dangerous suitor
- **Nausicaa**: Young princess of Scheria, beautiful, dignified, proper
- **Alcinous & Arete**: Ideal hosts of high xenia
- **Calypso**: Beautiful nymph, possessive yet ultimately obedient to Zeus
- **Circe**: Powerful enchantress, becomes host after being overcome
- **Polyphemus**: Savage one-eyed giant, son of Poseidon

Consistency rule: Once a character’s visual identity is established in early prompts, later prompts must not contradict it.
