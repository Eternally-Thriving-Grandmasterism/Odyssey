## Ithaca
- Rocky, rugged island
- Palace of Odysseus: Mycenaean-style megaron with central hearth, upper women’s quarters, courtyard, gates
- Harbor, swineherd’s hut on the slopes, farm of Laertes

## Pylos
- Sandy shore for the sacrifice to Poseidon
- Palace of Nestor: older, dignified Mycenaean hall

## Sparta
- Palace of Menelaus: richer, still Bronze Age in spirit, site of high xenia and lingering Trojan memory

## Ogygia (Calypso’s Island)
- Remote, lush, wooded, with a cave dwelling and beautiful natural surroundings
- Sense of isolation and suspended time

## Scheria (Phaeacia)
- Fertile, well-ordered land
- City and palace of Alcinous: the most splendid of the epic, with bronze elements, golden dogs, marvelous orchard — still within Homeric/Bronze Age imagination, not later classical architecture

## The Wanderings
- Cave of Polyphemus: vast, rough, pastoral yet terrible
- Circe’s hall: rich, magical, forested island
- Land of the Dead: misty, gloomy shore at the edge of the world
- Island of the Sirens, the strait of Scylla & Charybdis, Thrinacia (Cattle of the Sun): each distinct and true to Homer’s description

All locations must feel of one continuous mythic world — the world of Homeric epic in Bronze Age spirit.
